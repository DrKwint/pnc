\section{CIFAR-10 Hyperparameter Sweeps}
\label{app:cifar10-sweeps}

\subsection{Selection Protocol}
\label{app:cifar10-protocol}

All hyperparameters were selected per-method on a 10\% in-distribution validation
split held out from the CIFAR-10 training set. The remaining 90\% (45,000 images)
is used for training. Selection is by validation NLL after post-hoc temperature
scaling, with the temperature $T$ fit on the same 10\% split (one $T$ per
hyperparameter configuration). No OOD data is used for any tuning decision.

The shared training recipe across all methods using the PreAct ResNet-18 base is:
SGD with momentum 0.9 (Nesterov), initial lr $0.1$ with cosine decay and 5-epoch
warmup, weight decay $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$, batch size 128, 300 epochs, augmentation
\texttt{flipcrop+cutout(8)}, label smoothing 0.

Coverage notes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item LLLA, MC Dropout, Epinet, SWAG: only the headline hyperparameter was
        swept; secondary settings (e.g. SWAG \texttt{max\_rank}, Epinet
        \texttt{index\_dim}) were taken from defaults or the originating paper.
  \item PnC \texttt{bootstrap\_frac} was swept widely on validation-only runs
        but only $\{0.05, 0.5\}$ reached the full OpenOOD evaluation grid.
  \item PnC \texttt{lambda\_reg} was confirmed to have no measurable effect on
        validation NLL across five orders of magnitude, so it is not a useful
        knob and was not re-explored beyond the initial sweep.
  \item PnC \texttt{chunk\_size} affects only memory footprint, not the
        validation objective; it is set per-position to fit on an 8\,GB GPU.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{PnC Block-Position Sanity Check}
\label{app:pnc-position-sanity}

The original PnC block-position selection of stage 3, block 1 ("S3B1") was
made at seed 0 only, at a configuration that omitted bootstrap resampling.
Because bootstrap was added later (final $f_{\text{boot}} = 0.05$), we re-ran
the validation-only sweep at all 6 candidate positions
$(\text{stage}, \text{block}) \in \{1, 2, 3\} \times \{0, 1\}$ across three
seeds at the final configuration: $K = 20$, $\sigma = 25$, $\lambda = 10^{-3}$,
$f_{\text{boot}} = 0.05$, random directions, subset size 1024.

Table~\ref{tab:pnc-position-sanity} reports per-position validation NLL after
post-hoc temperature scaling. Position S3B0 (stage 3, block 0) achieves the
lowest validation NLL at every seed and the lowest mean. The originally
selected S3B1 ranks second; the gap is small (mean 0.1294 vs 0.1347, ${\sim}4\%$)
but consistent. The headline numbers reported in
Table~\ref{tab:ood_cifar10} use S3B0 with all other hyperparameters unchanged.

\subsection{MC Dropout Dropout-Rate Sweep (Stability Check)}
\label{app:mcd-sweep}

The CIFAR-10 MC Dropout configuration uses the literature-default dropout
rate of $0.1$. As a stability check we swept $\{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$ at
seed 0, training a fresh model for each value under the shared recipe
(300 epochs) and evaluating with $n = 32$ stochastic forward passes.
Table~\ref{tab:mcd-dropout-sweep} reports the per-rate validation and test
metrics at seed 0; by validation NLL the seed-0 winner is $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.2$.

To verify whether the seed-0 advantage replicates, we trained and evaluated
$p_{\text{drop}} = 0.2$ at two additional seeds and computed the full 3-seed
OpenOOD evaluation, alongside the existing $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.1$ baseline.
Table~\ref{tab:mcd-3seed} compares the two rates across all paper-table
metrics. The seed-0 validation gap collapses into noise: differences in
accuracy, NLL, near-AUROC, and far-AUROC are all well within the seed-to-seed
standard deviation, while ECE and FPR@95 are slightly worse at $0.2$ (notably
$\text{far FPR95}$ has a $3\times$ wider standard deviation at $0.2$).
We therefore retain $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.1$ in the headline paper table; the
selection by seed-0 validation NLL was not robust at three seeds.
